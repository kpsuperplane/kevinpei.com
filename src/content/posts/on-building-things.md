There's a version of every project that lives only in your head. It's clean. It works exactly as intended. The edge cases are handled gracefully and the code is — somehow — both simple and expressive.

Then you start building.

## The gap

The gap between the imagined thing and the built thing is where most of the interesting work happens. It's where you discover that your mental model was wrong, that the problem was different than you thought, that the constraint you ignored was actually load-bearing.

Most people treat this gap as failure. I've started treating it as the whole point.

## Constraints are information

Every time a project fights back — when the approach doesn't work, when the API is worse than the docs suggested, when the design breaks on mobile — it's telling you something. The question is whether you're listening.

The projects I'm most proud of are the ones where I changed direction at least once. Not because I gave up, but because I learned something that made a different direction obviously right.

## Finishing

The hardest part isn't starting. It isn't even the middle, when the project is ugly and half-working and you can't quite see the path to done.

The hardest part is the last 10%. When the thing mostly works and you have to decide: is this good enough to ship, or is there something important I'm still avoiding?

I've shipped things too early. I've also sat on things too long. I don't have a clean rule for this. What I have is a question I ask myself: *would I be embarrassed if someone I respect saw this?* If the answer is yes, there's more work to do.

If the answer is no, ship it.
